Our Founder

LTC John Michael Stacy, USA (Ret.)

Vietnam combat aviator. Distinguished Flying Cross Society member. 100% service-disabled. Three decades supporting the nation's largest defense primes.

Our company is led by Lieutenant Colonel John Michael Stacy, U.S. Army (Retired) — a Vietnam combat aviator, member of the Distinguished Flying Cross Society, and a 100% permanently and totally service-connected disabled veteran of twenty years of honorable Army service (1966–1986).

John flew O-1 Bird Dog reconnaissance missions with the 220th Reconnaissance Airplane Company (the "Catkillers") in support of the 1st and 3rd Marine Divisions in I Corps, Republic of Vietnam. He conducted visual reconnaissance over North Vietnam, adjusted naval gunfire from the USS New Jersey (BB-62), and controlled close air support under combat conditions as a Tactical Air Controller, Airborne.

After leaving active duty, John spent three decades on the other side of the federal mission — leading marketing, strategy, and business development for Bombardier (Canada Air) from 1990 to 2001, serving as Vice President at Burdeshaw Associates from 2002 to 2016, and as Senior Vice President at Strategic Marketing Innovations through 2018. Across those years, he led contract win strategies for Northrop Grumman, Lockheed Martin, Boeing, BAE Systems, and Raytheon.

He holds a B.S. in Business Administration from Auburn University and an M.S. in Systems Management from the University of Southern California.

Why this business exists

The next chapter of a fifty-year commitment.

After a lifetime of service — first flying for the country, then helping the largest defense companies in America win and deliver federal work — John saw the same problem again and again: agencies needed technology partners they could actually trust to put the mission first, not the invoice.

He started US Government Services LLC to be exactly that partner, and to create careers for the fellow veterans who already know what "mission first" means. This company is the next chapter of a fifty-year commitment to serving the United States.
